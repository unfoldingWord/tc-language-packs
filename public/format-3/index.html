For Bible translators, and the apps that serve them · In development, November 2026

A whole language’s translation helps, on any device, with no internet.

A language pack carries everything a translator needs for one language: a Bible for translators aligned word by word to the Hebrew and Greek, the notes, the questions, the key terms, the Academy articles, a lexicon and Bible stories. It is small enough to pass on a memory card, and it carries a map of the whole Bible.

Translation helps live online, in many places. Translators often don’t.

Many Bible translation teams work where the internet is slow, expensive or missing, on low-cost phones and old laptops. The helps they rely on are published online as dozens of separate collections. Before an app can show them, they have to be downloaded, read, and connected: each note matched to the exact Greek or Hebrew words it talks about, and to the words that translate them in the Bible.

7–9min

to prepare one language on a computer

Preparing the Spanish helps takes a computer 7 to 9 minutes and more than 1 GB of memory. A cheap phone cannot do it. Without internet, nothing can. A pack does that work once, on a server, and every device opens the result in seconds.

66books

that rarely talk to each other

The helps for each book are published on their own. A note in Titus knows nothing about Romans, so following a word, a figure of speech or a person across the Bible means having every book on hand and searching them one by one. On a phone with room for only a few books, that is out of reach.

One language, ready to read

Each piece arrives on its own, so a phone can carry one book or the whole Bible. The original Greek and Hebrew, and the map of the whole Bible, are shared by every language and travel once.

  • bookA book of the BibleIn the Bibles for translators, aligned word by word to the original, with their notes, questions and key-term links, each already placed in the text.
  • originalHebrew and GreekEvery word with its form, lemma, Strong number and morphology.
  • articlesKey terms and AcademyWord articles and Translation Academy, grouped, in sections that keep their names.
  • lexiconLexicon entriesMeanings of Hebrew and Greek words, in the language’s own lexicon.
  • storiesOpen Bible StoriesWith their notes, questions and word links.
  • mapThe map of the whole BibleWhere every original word, key term and Academy article appears, book by book and verse by verse. Next: the people, places and events of the Bible, in a module of their own.
  • catalogThe table of contentsWhich pieces exist, how big they are, what they need, and each resource’s license and credits.

Everything a translator needs, wherever they are

Work with no connection

Open a language on a phone or a computer and read the text, its alignment, notes, questions, articles and lexicon offline. Nothing is prepared on the device.

Pass it hand to hand

Move a language by SD card, USB or Bluetooth in one transfer file. An update carries only the pieces that changed.

Follow a word through the Bible

From any word of the original, see every book and verse where it appears, and, in the books on the device, how each Bible for translators rendered it.

Touch grace in Titus 2:11 → 156 places in 23 books.

See where an Academy article applies

Find every note that points to an article, book by book, and how many notes in this language use it.

Notes that point to Metaphor, across the whole Bible.

Know what is missing

The map tells “none” apart from “not on this device”, so an app can say what is out there without pretending it isn’t.

“And 22 more books, not on this device.”

Trust what arrives

Every piece is named by a fingerprint of its content and checks itself when opened. Building the same pack twice gives the same bytes.

Keep references working

Books, verses, words, notes and article sections keep their names from one release to the next, so a saved link or citation still resolves after an update.

Carry the credits

Each resource travels with its license and attribution, so any app can show who made it and how it may be used.

Coming with the maps

Find people, places and events

Every person, place and event of the Bible with all its verses and relations, and the passages each Bible story tells. Search people and places by the name your own Bible uses: the alignment gives each name in every language with an aligned text, and shows where one name was spelled two ways.

Abraham: his family, where he lived, every verse. “Abraham” in Genesis, “Abrahán” in Romans?

Take only what you need

A phone with little space should not carry what its translator will never open. The pack comes in layers: what everyone needs, what is light enough to come along by itself, and what an app offers with its size before downloading.

Always

  • Texts, helps and the originalThe Bibles for translators, notes, questions, articles, lexicon and the Hebrew and Greek.
  • The map of words and articlesWhere every word of the original, key term and Academy article appears.

Comes along by itself

  • TopicsPeople, places, events, Bible stories and book outlines. A module of its own, so a weekly update of its sources never makes a device download the rest again.

One format, open to every app

The format is written as an open specification, so an app does not need anyone else’s code to read it.

Translate

Servos

Offline-first app for translating a book of the Bible on phones and computers, with packs passed by card, USB or Bluetooth.

Study

tC Study

Bible reading and study with the translation helps, learning to load a language from packs, one book at a time.

Ask

Translation Helps MCP

Gives AI assistants the notes, terms and texts of a language, so one pack could serve both the translator and their assistant, even offline.

Build

Your app

A reader, an audio Bible, a checking tool or a study app can take the pieces it needs and ignore the rest.

Why another team can adopt it

  • Every piece has a published schema (CDDL) that a reader in any language can check against.
  • Two readers written apart, one in TypeScript and one in Python, must give the same output for every test case.
  • Written rules say what a minor version may add and what needs a new major version. Older readers skip what they do not know.

Built in phases, measured first

The format is being built now, in phases, and nothing is published yet.

  1. Done

    Phase 0 · Measure

    The design was tried on real packs before anything was written down. The sizes above come from it.

  2. Done

    Phase 1 · The container and the readers

    The specification, the builders, the TypeScript and Python readers, and the frozen test cases.

  3. In progress

    Phase 2 · The maps

    The map of the whole Bible and each language’s map: word counts, key terms, Academy usage and what each book holds. Then the topics: people, places, events and the passages of each Bible story, from sources already chosen.

  4. Planned

    Add-ons · Pictures, then maps

    Photos of animals and plants first, then the Bible story pictures, then maps drawn with names in each language.

  5. Next

    Phase 3 · Signatures

    So a pack passed from hand to hand can prove who made it, with no internet.
