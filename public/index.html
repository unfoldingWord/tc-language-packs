A language in one file

    Made once, opened anywhere

    The slow work happens once, on unfoldingWord's build server, each time a resource is published. Nobody waits for it on a phone, and nobody has to run it to use a pack.

    47 minutes, once, to prepare everything in English, on our server66 books in four Bibles with their notes, questions and key terms, every Translation Words and Academy article, and the lexicons.
    15 seconds to open a full packOnce, on your device, with no internet: every book, article and lexicon in it. One book takes less.
    2 ms for a verse's notesEvery time you read, already prepared.

    47 minutes, on our serverThe server downloads every English resource from Door43 and prepares it: four English Bibles of up to 31,000 verses each, every word linked to the Greek or Hebrew, and every translation note placed on the exact words it explains in the original and in each Bible. For English that is everything at once: 66 books in four Bibles with their notes, questions and key terms, 953 Translation Words and 242 Translation Academy articles, and the Greek and Hebrew lexicons. It takes 47 minutes and 15 GB of memory, more than most phones have. It runs again only when a resource changes, and then only for the books that changed.

    15 seconds, on your deviceOpening copies the prepared rows into the app's storage, once. The 15 seconds are for a full pack on a laptop: everything Latin American Spanish had (texts and helps of its six books, all its Translation Words and Academy articles, its lexicons) and the whole Greek and Hebrew Bible, 27 MB. One book takes less.

    2 ms, every readAfter that, each time someone opens a verse, its notes come back in 2 milliseconds.

    Preparing: all of English as one build on GitHub's server, October 2026. Opening: the full pack of Latin American Spanish with the originals (27 MB), the first language packed. Reading: translation-helps-mcp answering from a pack. Widths follow the first two; reading is too short to draw.

    Where the signal ends at the road

    Teams who translate into the languages of their own towns often work where the power line arrives and the data does not. The pack was made for them: everything the app reads is already on the device.

    It travels in the hand

    A pack is a file like any other. It goes the way a team already shares things.

    • Inside the app's installer, opened on first start.
    • On an SD card or a USB stick: a team that translates Titus carries 4.1 MB of English helps and Greek.
    • By Bluetooth or nearby sharing, phone to phone.
    • In a chat message: one book weighs less than a photo.
    • With internet, by parts: only what changed comes down.

    Who reads a pack

    • translation-helps-mcp ↗(opens in a new tab)

      The MCP server that gives AI assistants Bible texts and helps. Serves packs and parts to apps, and answers notes and word links from views made from a pack.

      In use
    • tC Study ↗(opens in a new tab)

      Shares the code that makes and stores packs, and the same cache keys. Its reading page cannot open a pack yet.

      Planned
    • Servos ↗(opens in a new tab)

      Idiomas Puentes' app for translating a book of the Bible by phone or laptop, in writing and by voice. Opens whole packs from a file or its installer, and downloads parts with internet. Reads every layer.

      In use
    • Your app or agent

      A reader, an audio Bible, a checking tool or an offline assistant. Take the layers you need; agent skills teach Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or Gemini CLI to read packs.

    Help build it

    tC Language Packs are open: the format, the code that makes and reads them, this site and the skills. Programmers and their agents are welcome to improve them.

    git clone https://github.com/unfoldingWord/tc-study
cd tc-study && bun install
cd packages/language-pack && bun test

    Working with a coding agent? Give it the developer skill first: it knows the format, the keys, the versions and the parts, and can check what it builds against real packs.

    Open issuesHow to contribute
    • In progressThe word index

      Find every verse where a Greek or Hebrew word appears, in every book, even books not on the device. Packages language-pack and resource-pipeline.

    • OpenPacks that outlive the code

      Today a change in the code that prepares a layer makes older packs unreadable for it. A stable level of source and views would let teams keep sharing packs across app versions.

    • OpenA public schema

      JSON Schema for the manifest, the parts list and each row, so apps in any language can check a pack.

    • OpenSigned packs

      A pack passed by hand cannot yet prove who made it. A signature that checks with no internet.

    • OpentC Study opens packs

      tC Study shares the code but its reading page still needs the network to open a language.

    • OpenAnswers with no internet

      translation-helps-mcp reading packs from a folder, so an assistant can answer offline.

    • OpenMore languages

      Any language with an aligned literal text marked tc-ready on Door43 can be packed. Try yours and report what breaks.

    Make one yourself

    The command runs tC Study's own pipeline on your computer. A large language like English is built as parts, a book at a time; a small one fits in one pack. The next page explains every flag.

    Prepare a language pack

    bun packages/resource-pipeline/cli/publish-language.ts --parts --lang en --work ./work --macula <taller>/public --lexicons-ref master --no-upload
